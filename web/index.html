Follow a satellite

Pick a satellite and the camera flies with it: drag to circle around it, scroll to come closer. In Whole Earth, drag to turn the Earth and scroll to zoom right down to the ground, Ctrl + drag (or two fingers) to tilt the view. The surface sharpens as you zoom in: only the part you look at is loaded. Earth, orbits, heights and the radar beam are to scale; the satellite models are simple sketches enlarged about 4,000 times. Imagery: NASA Blue Marble and Landsat mosaic. Orbits: CelesTrak.

Satellites around Earth right now

Drag the globe to turn it and look at the other side of the planet; scroll or use + and − to zoom in on the radar beam. Earth, the satellites' heights and the radar beam are drawn to scale; only the satellite pictures are enlarged. The arrow in front of each satellite shows where it is flying. Solid line: last 50 minutes, dashed: next 50. NISAR's beam is a thin fan, about 240 km wide across the track; the radar does not record all the time. Positions are computed from CelesTrak orbital elements; Earth image: NASA Blue Marble.

Welcome to Dancing Earth

Radar on the NISAR satellite sees Earth's surface through cloud, smoke and darkness. Here you can watch the surface change, and hear it.

  1. Pick a topic at the top: Nature (floods, fires, hurricanes) or Human activity (forest clearing, farming), then a place.
  2. Press Play to step through every satellite pass and hear the change as music.
  3. Try Optical to see what a camera saw the same day, often just cloud, and drag the slider.
  4. Open Live world for today's events, satellites overhead, and a radar image of any place on land.
