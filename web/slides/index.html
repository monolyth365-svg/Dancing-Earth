The idea

Radar sees floods and fires through cloud and smoke. But a radar image is hard to read.

1 · Show the change

A coloured map marks where the surface changed, with a before / after slider and a timeline of every satellite pass.

2 · Say it in numbers

One plain sentence tells what happened, with square kilometres and a trend. The data can be downloaded as CSV.

3 · Let it play

Each image becomes music: brightness sets the pitch, the amount of change sets the loudness. Places that change sound different from places that stay still.

Main topic: water (floods and lakes). Also wildfires, forest clearing and farm fields.

The project

One screen: map, story, numbers, sound

  • Layers HH, HV, Change and Optical: a camera photo of the same day, to compare with radar
  • Before / after slider
  • What happened sentence made from the data
  • Timeline with Play and sound
  • Click a spot for its own time series
  • CSV export and a link to share the exact view
  • Live world: today's events from NASA, satellites overhead, and the newest NISAR image of any place on land, with the change shown and played

Works on a phone, with a keyboard, and has text for screen readers.

How it is used

Pick a place, press Play, read the answer

  1. Choose a place: lake, wildfire, forest or farmland.
  2. Read the sentence and the three key numbers.
  3. Press Play to watch and hear every pass.
  4. Drag Before / after to reveal what changed.
  5. Click the map to follow one exact spot.
  6. Download CSV or copy the link to share.

Flood and fire managers

How much water is there now, and is it rising? Where inside the fire perimeter was woody vegetation really lost?

Scientists

Numbers per pass, thresholds, granule names, CSV, and a method section with the limits.

Teachers and the public

A short story for each place, a slider, and music that makes change easy to notice.

What the data shows

Water: one lake fills, another empties

1,043 → 1,572 km² of open water, Tonlé Sap

June to October 2026. Over the same months Poyang Lake in China shrank from 2,204 to 644 km². Lesson from the data: wind makes water bright in HH but not in HV, so water is mapped from HV. The exact area depends on the threshold; the trend does not.

Fire: Shingle Fire burn scar

96 % of detected loss is inside the official perimeter

104 km² of vegetation loss, compared with the average of the passes before the fire. The perimeter is used only as an independent check.

An honest limit: Big Grass Fire

5–9 % of the burn area is visible

A 2,350 km² grass fire that L-band radar barely sees. The app shows it and explains why, instead of hiding it.

Role of NASA data

Every picture, number and note comes from NISAR

  • NISAR L2 GCOV (NASA-ISRO L-band radar), from the NASA ASF DAAC: 77 satellite passes over 6 places, HH and HV polarization.
  • NASA EONET: live map of current events; also used to find the 2026 Oregon wildfires.
  • NISAR quick-look images from ASF: read live for any place on Earth.
  • NASA GIBS: daily VIIRS true-colour photos to show the clouds radar sees through, and the Blue Marble basemap.
  • NASA Earthdata SAR learning pages and the NISAR Data User Guide: the physics in the stories.

Other data: official fire perimeters from NIFC (US National Interagency Fire Center), used to check the fire result; satellite orbits from CelesTrak.

How the data is processed

  1. Read only a small window of each 1.8 GB file over the internet.
  2. Average in linear power, then convert to dB.
  3. Smooth to reduce speckle and compare with a reference pass.
  4. Count area above a threshold: open water, vegetation loss.
  5. Turn each image into 16 notes for the music.

Open source · use of AI · links

Free to use, honest about how it was made

Open source, MIT licence. Add a new place by adding its coordinates to one file.

Use of AI. The idea and the decisions are human: a map of surface change, water first, plus fires, plus music from the change. An AI assistant (Claude by Anthropic) wrote most of the code and drafted the English text. No AI-generated images, video or audio are used.

Limits. A drop in radar signal is a hint, not proof. Grass fires and water under trees are mostly invisible. Area numbers are approximate.
